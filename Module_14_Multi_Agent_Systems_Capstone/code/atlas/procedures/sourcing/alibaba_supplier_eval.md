# Alibaba Supplier Evaluation, Side Shop

This procedure evaluates one Alibaba supplier, or one specific listing, for Side Shop, Trilok's makeup-vanity import business. Follow it in order. Produce a written evaluation, store the findings in semantic memory, and hand the CEO a clear verdict.

## What you are given

Trilok forwards an Alibaba supplier or product URL, sometimes with notes. You may instead be given details he pasted directly: price, MOQ, certifications, the supplier's profile, reviews, message history. Work with what you have. When a fact you need is missing, say so plainly rather than assuming it.

## Step 1: Compliance check, the hard requirements

Side Shop sells into the United States, so three requirements are non-negotiable.

1. CARB Phase 2. The composite wood in the vanity (particleboard, MDF, plywood) must meet California Air Resources Board Phase 2 formaldehyde emission limits.
2. EPA TSCA Title VI. The federal formaldehyde standard for composite wood products. A supplier compliant with one is usually compliant with the other, but confirm each on its own.
3. 110-120V US socket. Any vanity with lights, outlets, or USB ports must ship with US-standard 110-120V wiring and plugs, not 220-240V.

For each requirement, record one status: confirmed (the supplier provided a certificate, a test report, or a clear written statement), claimed (the supplier says yes but offered no evidence), or unconfirmed (no information either way).

A compliance gap does not reject a supplier on its own. If any requirement is anything other than confirmed, the verdict is at most "Needs your review", and you state exactly which requirement is short and what evidence would close it. Trilok decides whether to proceed. Never quietly pass a supplier whose compliance you could not confirm.

## Step 2: Scored evaluation

Score the supplier out of 100 across five factors. Unit price carries the most weight, because margin is the priority for Side Shop.

Unit price and landed cost, 40 points. Judge the per-unit cost delivered, not just the listed price. Account for shipping and for the MOQ. Lower landed cost scores higher. If you cannot estimate landed cost from what you were given, score what you can and flag the gap.

Reputation and trust, 25 points. The external evidence that this is a real, reliable supplier. Weigh whether they hold Alibaba "Verified Supplier" status; whether the order would be covered by Alibaba Trade Assurance or an equivalent platform guarantee; their review score and the number of reviews; how many years they have been on the platform; the transaction history and response rate shown on their profile; and their presence beyond Alibaba, from the online-presence check in Step 2a. A verified supplier with Trade Assurance, strong reviews, and years of history scores high. Thin, inconsistent, or missing signals score low.

Supplier communication, 15 points. Responsiveness, clarity, willingness to share certificates and samples, and quality of written English. This is how the supplier deals with you, as distinct from their track record.

MOQ fit, 10 points. Side Shop can take on up to 50 units per supplier. An MOQ of 50 or below scores full or near-full marks. 51 to 100 scores partial. Above 100 scores low. Record the exact MOQ.

Lead time, 10 points. Order placed to delivered. Up to 90 days is acceptable. 45 days or less is excellent, 46 to 90 is fine, above 90 loses most of these points. Record the quoted lead time.

Sum the five into a score out of 100.

### Step 2a: Online presence check

Look the supplier up beyond their Alibaba profile. Search for the company name and check for a real company website, a consistent identity across sites, listings on other B2B marketplaces, and any complaints, scam reports, or other red flags. A supplier with a coherent, verifiable presence is a safer bet; one that exists only as a thin Alibaba page is a risk. Fold what you find into the reputation and trust score, and state plainly what you checked and what you found. If you have no way to search the web, say so: assess reputation from the Alibaba profile alone and flag clearly that the external online presence was not independently checked.

## Step 3: Verdict

Combine the compliance status and the score into one verdict.

Recommended: all three hard requirements confirmed, and score 70 or above.

Needs your review: any hard requirement not confirmed, or score between 50 and 69. State plainly what holds it back and what Trilok would need to decide.

Not recommended: all three hard requirements confirmed, but score below 50.

A compliance gap always caps the verdict at "Needs your review", even when the score is high. Trilok makes the call on compliance, not you.

## Step 4: Record and report

Write the findings to semantic memory as facts: the supplier name, the score, the verdict, the compliance status of each hard requirement, the reputation signals (verified status, Trade Assurance, reviews, years active), the MOQ, the lead time, and the landed-cost estimate. Word each fact so it is still useful months from now, when another supplier is weighed against this one.

Then give the CEO a short, plain summary: the verdict first, then the score, the one or two things that drove it, and anything that needs Trilok's decision. Lead with what matters. If the listing was missing information you needed, name what was missing.

## Notes

CARB Phase 2 and TSCA Title VI both concern formaldehyde from composite wood. A solid-wood vanity can sidestep both, but confirm the material rather than assuming it. A CARB certificate carries an identifier that can be checked against the CARB website. Treat a supplier's refusal to provide certificates as a real negative, both on the communication factor and on reputation. Alibaba Trade Assurance matters because it backs the order if the supplier fails to deliver as agreed; an order placed outside it carries that risk on Trilok directly.
